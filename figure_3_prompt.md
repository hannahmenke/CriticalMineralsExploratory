# Figure 3 Image Generation Prompt

Create a clean, publication-quality workflow diagram showing how correlative Digital Rock Physics connects ore samples to process decisions through multimodal imaging, chemistry, AI segmentation, and simulation.

Important rendering constraints:
- The background must be pure white (#FFFFFF) across the entire image.
- The figure should look like it was built manually in Adobe Illustrator: crisp vector shapes, precise alignment, clean strokes, flat fills, consistent typography, and no raster/photo texture.
- Do not render a figure title.
- Do not render a figure caption.
- Do not render a figure number.
- Do not include journal-style caption text inside the image.
- Internal scientific labels and short annotations are allowed.
- Panel letters or stage numbers are allowed if useful.

Overall composition:
- Landscape orientation, approximately 16:9.
- Pure white background (#FFFFFF), with no off-white tint, paper texture, gradient, vignette, border, or background pattern.
- Left-to-right workflow with six major stages.
- Use clean Adobe Illustrator-style vector icons and blocks.
- Use muted colours with consistent meaning:
  - grey or brown for physical samples;
  - blue for X-ray and CT structure;
  - green for chemical and elemental mapping;
  - purple for AI and data processing;
  - orange for modelling and simulation;
  - teal for process decisions.
- Keep all labels clear and readable.
- Avoid photorealistic instruments.
- Use simplified schematic icons rather than detailed laboratory equipment.

Stage 1: Representative material
- Show three small input icons grouped together:
  - "ore core / drill chip"
  - "tailings particle"
  - "black mass / recycling feedstock"
- Use small stylised particles or cylinders.
- Label the group "representative critical-mineral sample".

Stage 2: 3D structure
- Show a simplified micro-CT scanner or X-ray beam passing through a sample.
- Show output as a small 3D voxel cube with pores, grains, and fractures.
- Include short internal labels:
  - "macro-CT"
  - "micro-CT"
  - "nano-CT"
  - "grain geometry"
  - "pore and fracture network"

Stage 3: Correlative chemistry
- Show the same volume being mapped with coloured elemental overlays.
- Include small method labels:
  - "SEM-EDX"
  - "XRF-CT"
  - "XANES-CT"
  - "XRD-CT"
- Show coloured maps registered onto the 3D volume.
- Include label: "mineral identity, elemental distribution, chemical state".
- Add a visual registration marker or alignment grid to show that structural and chemical datasets are co-registered.

Stage 4: AI-ready interpretation
- Show the fused dataset entering a segmentation and model block.
- Include stylised neural-network nodes, but keep them subtle.
- Show output as a labelled segmented volume with distinct classes:
  - "target mineral"
  - "gangue"
  - "pores / fractures"
  - "alteration products"
- Include short internal labels:
  - "AI segmentation"
  - "uncertainty"
  - "metadata"
  - "benchmark dataset"
- Include a small quality-control checkmark labelled "validated labels".

Stage 5: Image-based simulation
- Show the segmented 3D volume turning into a computational mesh or pore-network model.
- Include simple flow arrows through pores and reaction fronts around mineral grains.
- Include short internal labels:
  - "reactive transport"
  - "permeability"
  - "dissolution / precipitation"
  - "liberation model"
  - "surrogate model"
- Add a small plot showing model prediction versus experiment, with simple lines and no detailed numbers.

Stage 6: Decision support
- Show final outputs as a compact decision panel with four boxes:
  - "drill-target ranking"
  - "comminution target"
  - "leach or DLE operating window"
  - "recycling / tailings route selection"
- Include an arrow from this decision panel back to Stage 1 labelled "new samples / iterative learning", forming a feedback loop under the workflow.

Lower data-infrastructure band:
- Include a horizontal band spanning stages 2 through 6.
- Label the band "Digital Ore Passport / shared data infrastructure".
- The band should contain small but readable terms:
  - "image volumes"
  - "chemical maps"
  - "process metadata"
  - "uncertainty"
  - "model-ready formats"

Style requirements:
- Pure white canvas only.
- Adobe Illustrator-style scientific vector artwork.
- The figure should read as a clear systems workflow, not an equipment catalogue.
- Use arrows to show sequence and feedback.
- The diagram should be dense enough for expert readers but still visually clean.
- No corporate logos.
- No facility names.
- No realistic photos.
- Output should look like a high-quality review-paper schematic.

Suggested manuscript caption, not to be rendered in the image:

Correlative Digital Rock Physics workflow linking representative critical-mineral samples to process decisions. Multiscale tomography provides 3D structure, correlative chemical methods identify mineralogy and chemical state, AI segmentation creates model-ready volumes, and image-based simulation translates those observations into decision support.
