# Figure 4 Image Generation Prompt

Create a detailed scientific conceptual diagram of an integrated in situ geo-reactive imaging platform for critical-mineral processing experiments, showing simultaneous 3D imaging, reactive flow, outlet chemistry, AI/data processing, and process optimisation.

Important rendering constraints:
- The background must be pure white (#FFFFFF) across the entire image.
- The figure should look like it was built manually in Adobe Illustrator: crisp vector shapes, precise alignment, clean strokes, flat fills, consistent typography, and no raster/photo texture.
- Do not render a figure title.
- Do not render a figure caption.
- Do not render a figure number.
- Do not include journal-style caption text inside the image.
- Internal scientific labels and short annotations are allowed.
- Panel letters or inset labels are allowed if useful.

Overall composition:
- Landscape orientation, approximately 16:9.
- Pure white background (#FFFFFF), with no off-white tint, paper texture, gradient, vignette, border, or background pattern.
- Central focus: a flow-through sample cell inside an imaging beamline or scanner.
- Surrounding modules arranged logically around the central cell.
- Use clean Adobe Illustrator-style vector schematic drawing, not photorealistic rendering.
- Use a restrained scientific colour scheme:
  - steel grey for hardware;
  - blue for imaging beam and data;
  - green for chemistry and fluid analytics;
  - orange or red for reaction fronts and dissolved metals;
  - teal for decision outputs.
- All labels must be readable and scientifically accurate.
- Avoid clutter, while including enough detail to show why this is an integrated platform.

Central element:
- Draw a cylindrical or rectangular transparent flow cell containing a porous ore, tailings, sorbent, or black-mass sample.
- Inside the sample, show mineral grains, pores, fractures, and a moving reaction front.
- Show inlet fluid entering from the left and outlet fluid leaving on the right.
- Label the central cell:
  - "in situ flow-through sample cell"
  - "ore / tailings / sorbent / black mass"
  - "controlled flow, temperature, pressure, and mechanical loading"
- Show optional compression platens or a confining sleeve around the sample to indicate mechanical loading.

Left side: influent system
- Draw reagent reservoirs and pumps feeding the sample.
- Include short labels:
  - "brine"
  - "acidic or alkaline lixiviant"
  - "recycling leachate"
  - "controlled flow rate"
- Use arrows showing fluid entering the cell.
- Include small icons for temperature and pressure control.

Top and centre: imaging system
- Show an X-ray beam or imaging field passing through the sample.
- Include short labels:
  - "time-resolved X-ray CT"
  - "XRF / XANES / XRD mapping"
  - "MRI / PET where appropriate"
- Show a detector on the opposite side of the sample.
- From the detector, draw an arrow to a reconstructed 3D image volume.
- The reconstructed volume should show pores, grains, and colour-coded chemical or reactive zones.
- Include short labels around the reconstructed volume:
  - "3D texture"
  - "mineral chemistry"
  - "pore-network evolution"
  - "reaction-front tracking"

Right side: effluent analytics
- Show outlet fluid flowing into inline analytical instruments.
- Include labelled modules:
  - "ICP-OES / ICP-MS"
  - "ion chromatography"
  - "pH / Eh / conductivity"
  - "optical spectroscopy"
- Show a small effluent concentration plot labelled "released species vs time".
- Use coloured curves for generic "target metal" and "gangue element".
- Avoid overcrowding the plot.
- Label this section "synchronised outlet-fluid chemistry".

Bottom: data integration and modelling
- Draw a horizontal computational layer beneath the experiment.
- Inputs from imaging and effluent analytics should feed into this layer.
- Include modules:
  - "AI segmentation"
  - "uncertainty-aware registration"
  - "reactive transport simulation"
  - "surrogate model"
  - "experiment design"
- Show CPU/HPC and GPU icons or simplified server blocks.
- Include short labels:
  - "GPU inference"
  - "CPU/HPC reconstruction and simulation"
- Keep this visually compact.

Bottom right: process decisions
- Show final output boxes:
  - "leach contact efficiency"
  - "permeability management"
  - "selective dissolution"
  - "sorbent degradation / fouling"
  - "operating-window selection"
- Add a feedback arrow from process decisions back to the influent system labelled:
  - "adjust flow rate, chemistry, temperature, pressure, or cycle time"
- This feedback loop should clearly communicate that the platform supports optimisation rather than one-off observation.

Optional small inset:
- Include a magnified inset of the sample interior showing:
  - a target mineral grain dissolving;
  - precipitation blocking a pore throat;
  - preferential flow bypassing part of the sample.
- Include short labels:
  - "dissolution"
  - "precipitation / pore blockage"
  - "preferential flow"
- This inset should visually support the paper's argument that bulk assays alone miss spatially localised process failures.

Style requirements:
- Pure white canvas only.
- Adobe Illustrator-style scientific vector artwork.
- No facility logos.
- No real brand names.
- Do not show human operators.
- No dark background.
- Use clean arrows and modular layout.
- The figure should look like a serious scientific schematic suitable for a review article or policy white paper.

Suggested manuscript caption, not to be rendered in the image:

Conceptual design for an integrated geo-reactive DRP platform. Time-resolved imaging and correlative chemical mapping observe structural and mineralogical change inside the sample, while synchronised outlet-fluid analytics quantify released species. AI segmentation and reactive-transport modelling convert these linked measurements into process decisions for leaching, DLE, tailings reprocessing, and recycling.
