# Journal Fit Comparison: Resources Policy vs Environmental Science & Policy

Date checked: 2026-03-25

## Short Recommendation

If the manuscript stays close to its current form, `Resources Policy` is the better first submission target.

`Environmental Science & Policy` is still plausible, but it would require a more substantial reframing toward environmental governance, science-policy infrastructure, and broader public-interest implications.

## Quick Comparison

| Dimension | Resources Policy | Environmental Science & Policy |
|---|---|---|
| Overall fit to current manuscript | Strong | Moderate |
| Best audience match | Critical minerals, mining policy, resource strategy, industry-facing policy | Environmental policy scholars, science-policy community, broader sustainability policy readership |
| Current listed IF | No current IF shown on the ScienceDirect insights page | 5.2 |
| Current listed CiteScore | 17.0 | 12.5 |
| Broad publisher base | Elsevier / Pergamon, EU-facing ecosystem | Elsevier, EU-facing ecosystem |
| Public-page article positioning | Strong on minerals policy and economics | Explicitly accepts perspectives, reviews, letters, and research papers |
| Rewriting required | Limited to moderate | Moderate to substantial |

## Why Resources Policy Fits Better

- The paper is already centered on `critical minerals`, `processing infrastructure`, `recycling`, `strategic capability`, and `public investment`.
- That maps directly onto the journal's stated focus on policy and economics around mineral and fossil-fuel extraction, production, and use.
- The manuscript already speaks to a mixed audience of academia, government, and industry, which aligns well with the journal.
- The current paper reads more naturally as a `resource strategy / infrastructure policy` argument than as a broader environmental-policy intervention.

## Risks With Resources Policy

- Some sections may still read as too method-led unless they are tightly linked to policy outcomes.
- The DRP/facility discussion should remain connected to `supply security`, `resource efficiency`, `waste valorization`, and `pre-competitive infrastructure`.
- The journal's public-facing materials emphasize policy/economics more than a dedicated "perspective" identity, so the paper should read as a serious policy article rather than a white paper with citations.

## Changes Recommended Before Submitting to Resources Policy

- Reframe the central claim as: `DRP is strategic infrastructure for critical-mineral supply, process efficiency, and secondary resource recovery`.
- Tighten the abstract so the policy and investment argument appears before the technical detail.
- Add one short paragraph explaining why public co-investment is justified:
  common infrastructure, shared standards, benchmark data, and de-risking of industrial adoption.
- Keep the facility argument, but tie each proposed investment to a concrete policy payoff.
- End with a policy action sequence rather than a general scientific-opportunity conclusion.

## Why Environmental Science & Policy Could Work

- The journal explicitly presents itself as covering the relationship between environmental science, policy, and society.
- It explicitly accepts `perspectives`, which suits the genre better on paper.
- It is a better venue if the manuscript is reframed as a contribution to `science-policy infrastructure`, `environmental governance`, and `public-interest measurement systems`.

## Risks With Environmental Science & Policy

- In its current form, the manuscript is still too sector-specific and internally technical.
- The paper is stronger on critical-minerals strategy than on environmental-policy theory or governance.
- Without reframing, the manuscript may look like a specialized mining-policy piece rather than a broader environmental-science-and-policy contribution.
- The journal page currently lists a 7% acceptance rate, so the fit likely needs to be sharper.

## Changes Recommended Before Submitting to Environmental Science & Policy

- Reframe DRP as `environmental decision infrastructure`, not only critical-minerals infrastructure.
- Explain more explicitly how improved pore-scale and correlative measurements change environmental outcomes:
  lower waste, lower energy intensity, better reagent use, improved tailings stewardship, and more defensible recycling pathways.
- Add explicit policy concepts such as:
  `knowledge infrastructures`, `public goods`, `standards`, `mission-oriented investment`, and `regulatory accountability`.
- Reduce some field-specific technical detail and replace it with a clearer science-policy bottleneck.
- Add a paragraph on governance and public-interest implications:
  who controls the data, who benefits from infrastructure investments, and how standards should be governed.

## Practical Decision Rule

Choose `Resources Policy` if:

- you want the least rewriting
- you want the paper to remain clearly about critical minerals
- you want readers in mining, resource strategy, and industrial policy

Choose `Environmental Science & Policy` if:

- you are willing to substantially reframe the paper
- you want a broader environmental-policy audience
- you want the paper to read as a science-policy contribution rather than mainly a minerals strategy piece

## Bottom Line

`Resources Policy` is the better fit for the manuscript as it currently exists.

`Environmental Science & Policy` is the stronger option only if the paper is intentionally rewritten to foreground environmental governance, science-policy infrastructure, and societal implications.
