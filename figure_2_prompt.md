# Figure 2 Image Generation Prompt

Create a clean, publication-quality scientific conceptual diagram comparing conventional 2D automated mineralogy with 3D Digital Rock Physics for critical-mineral ore characterisation.

Important rendering constraints:
- The background must be pure white (#FFFFFF) across the entire image.
- The figure should look like it was built manually in Adobe Illustrator: crisp vector shapes, precise alignment, clean strokes, flat fills, consistent typography, and no raster/photo texture.
- Do not render a figure title.
- Do not render a figure caption.
- Do not render a figure number.
- Do not include journal-style caption text inside the image.
- Internal scientific labels and short annotations are allowed.
- Panel letters such as A, B, and C are allowed.

Overall composition:
- Landscape orientation, approximately 16:9.
- Pure white background (#FFFFFF), with no off-white tint, paper texture, gradient, vignette, border, or background pattern.
- Three main vertical panels arranged left to right.
- Use restrained scientific colours: charcoal grey for outlines and text, muted teal for target critical-mineral grains, pale orange for gangue minerals, slate blue for pores and fracture space, and light grey for host matrix.
- Use crisp Adobe Illustrator-style vector graphics, not photorealistic rendering.
- Avoid decorative backgrounds, gradients, shadows, 3D gimmicks, and excessive texture.
- All labels must be large, sharp, and readable in a journal figure.

Panel A content:
- Show a polished 2D section or polished mount as a flat circular or rectangular slice.
- Inside the section, show several mineral grains in different colours.
- Some teal target-mineral grains should appear isolated and apparently liberated in the 2D slice.
- Include short internal labels:
  - "SEM-EDX"
  - "QEMSCAN / MLA / TIMA"
  - "2D mineral ID"
  - "apparent liberation"
  - "section-based association"
- Add a small annotation arrow pointing to an isolated teal grain: "appears liberated in section".
- Make clear this is a flat slice, not a volume.
- Put panel letter "A" in the upper-left corner.

Panel B content:
- Show a semi-transparent 3D ore particle or cube-like volume corresponding to the same material.
- Use a cutaway or translucent rendering so internal grains are visible.
- Show that some teal critical-mineral grains that looked liberated in 2D are actually connected to or enclosed by orange or grey gangue in 3D.
- Include internal grain topology, connected inclusions, locked grains, middling particles, and small fractures or pore networks.
- Include short internal labels:
  - "X-ray micro-CT"
  - "nano-CT"
  - "true 3D grain topology"
  - "locked, liberated, and middling volumes"
  - "connectivity and accessibility"
- Add a small highlighted 2D slice plane passing through the 3D particle, visually linking Panel A to Panel B.
- Include an arrow from the 2D slice plane to the full 3D volume labelled "section expanded to volume".
- Put panel letter "B" in the upper-left corner.

Panel C content:
- Show a simplified decision-output box or dashboard.
- Include four stacked metric rows with small icons:
  - "true liberation volume"
  - "mineral association"
  - "grain-size distribution"
  - "connected pathways"
- Show arrows leading from those metrics to three process decisions:
  - "grind size"
  - "flowsheet design"
  - "recovery estimate"
- Include a subtle comparison:
  - "2D estimate may overstate liberation"
  - "3D DRP constrains processing risk"
- Use a simple split mini-bar chart showing "apparent 2D liberation" higher than "true 3D liberation".
- Do not include exact numerical values unless they remain clearly legible.
- The panel should communicate that 2D mineralogy is valuable for rapid mineral identification, while 3D DRP adds volumetric and connectivity information needed for processing decisions.
- Put panel letter "C" in the upper-left corner.

Style requirements:
- Pure white canvas only.
- Adobe Illustrator-style scientific vector artwork.
- Scientific editorial style suitable for a white paper or review article.
- No people.
- No laboratory room scene.
- No mining equipment.
- No unreadable microtext.
- Use simple arrows and clean panel dividers.
- Output should look like a polished Nature Reviews or Minerals Engineering conceptual figure.

Suggested manuscript caption, not to be rendered in the image:

Conceptual comparison of 2D automated mineralogy and 3D Digital Rock Physics. Section-based methods provide rapid mineral identification and association mapping, while volumetric imaging resolves true grain topology, locked and middling particles, connected pathways, and the processing decisions controlled by those features.
