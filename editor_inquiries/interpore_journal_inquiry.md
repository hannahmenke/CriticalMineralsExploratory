# InterPore Journal Inquiry

Subject: Pre-submission inquiry: Opinion Piece on digital rock physics for critical minerals

Dear Ms Lenz and Professor Shokri,

I am writing to ask whether *InterPore Journal* would welcome an Opinion Piece, or another appropriate perspective-style contribution, tentatively titled:

“Unlocking the Microscale: How Digital Rock Physics Can Secure Supply Chains for UK and European Critical Minerals”

The proposed paper argues that the digital rock physics and porous media community has a distinctive and urgent role to play in critical-mineral supply security. It links core InterPore themes, including multiscale imaging, correlative chemistry, pore-scale modelling, reactive transport, AI-enabled image analysis, and engineered porous materials, to major challenges in ore characterisation, liberation prediction, heap and in-situ leaching, direct lithium extraction, tailings valorisation, and battery recycling.

We see this as a strong fit for *InterPore Journal* because the paper positions porous media science as an enabling discipline for a major energy-transition bottleneck. Critical minerals are often discussed as a mining, processing, or policy problem, but many of the decisive uncertainties are fundamentally porous-media questions: texture, connectivity, multiphase transport, reactive pathways, evolving permeability, sorbent pore architecture, and scale bridging from images to process decisions. The paper therefore aims to make a clear case for why this topic belongs at the centre of the porous media research agenda.

The author team brings together complementary perspectives from digital rock physics, porous media imaging, critical-minerals policy, and energy-transition analysis: Hannah P. Menke, Heriot-Watt University; Alessio Scanziani, International Energy Agency; and Maja Rücker, Eindhoven University of Technology and the Max Planck Institute for Polymer Research.

We believe the article would be timely for *InterPore Journal* readers because critical-mineral security is becoming a defining applied challenge for the energy transition, and the porous media community already has much of the scientific infrastructure needed to contribute. The manuscript sets out an agenda for translational demonstrator projects, shared benchmark datasets, and integrated geo-reactive imaging platforms that would connect porous media methods more directly to mineral recovery, recycling, and low-footprint separation technologies.

This manuscript is not under formal consideration elsewhere; we are making fit inquiries before choosing a submission target.

Would this topic be of interest to the journal as an Opinion Piece or another suitable article category?

Best regards,  
Hannah P. Menke
