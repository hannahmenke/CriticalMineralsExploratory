# Resources Policy Inquiry

Subject: Pre-submission inquiry: Perspective on digital rock physics and critical minerals policy

Dear Professor Lei and Professor Vivoda,

I am writing to ask whether *Resources Policy* would welcome a Perspective submission tentatively titled:

“Unlocking the Microscale: How Digital Rock Physics Can Secure Supply Chains for UK and European Critical Minerals”

The proposed paper argues that digital rock physics should be understood as strategic infrastructure for critical-mineral supply security, resource efficiency, and circularity. It connects 3D imaging, correlative chemistry, AI-enabled image analysis, and pore-scale modelling to policy-relevant bottlenecks in ore characterisation, liberation prediction, leaching, direct lithium extraction, mine-waste valorisation, and battery recycling.

We see this as a strong fit for *Resources Policy* because the paper is not primarily a methods review. Its central contribution is a resource-policy argument: UK and European critical-minerals strategies will depend not only on permitting, project designation, and headline supply targets, but also on the pre-competitive measurement, data, and modelling infrastructure needed to turn complex regional resources into viable, lower-impact supply chains. The paper therefore speaks directly to strategic minerals and their supply, mineral governance, sustainable resource development, and the policy design of shared research infrastructure.

The author team brings together expertise from digital rock physics, porous media imaging, critical-minerals policy, and energy-transition analysis: Hannah P. Menke, Heriot-Watt University; Alessio Scanziani, International Energy Agency; and Maja Rücker, Eindhoven University of Technology and the Max Planck Institute for Polymer Research.

We believe the paper would be timely for *Resources Policy* readers because critical-mineral security is moving rapidly from target-setting to implementation. The manuscript offers a concrete agenda for translational demonstrator projects, shared data standards, and a federated digital ore infrastructure that could help de-risk domestic extraction, processing, and recycling.

This manuscript is not under formal consideration elsewhere; we are making fit inquiries before choosing a submission target.

Would this topic be of interest to the journal as a Perspective?

Best regards,  
Hannah P. Menke
